Kick a football and it flies. Catch it and it stops. Squeeze it and it changes shape. Each time, **something pushed or pulled the ball**. That push or pull is a **force**.

<KeyIdea>
A **force** is a push or a pull. Nothing starts moving, stops, speeds up, slows down, turns or changes shape unless a force acts on it.
</KeyIdea>

## What forces can do

A force can:

- **start** something moving (pushing a swing);
- **stop** something (a goalkeeper catching a ball);
- make something **faster or slower** (pedalling harder, braking);
- **change its direction** (a tennis racket sending the ball back);
- **change its shape** (squashing a lump of clay).

## Every force has a size and a direction

"Push the door" is not enough information. *How hard?* And *which way?* Pushing a door towards its hinges does nothing useful. Pushing near the handle opens it easily.

So scientists draw a force as an **arrow**:

- the arrow points the way the force acts (its **direction**);
- the longer the arrow, the bigger the force (its **size**).

## Measuring forces: the newton

We measure force in **newtons**. The short form is **N**. The unit is named after Isaac Newton, the scientist who worked out the rules of how forces make things move.

A good way to feel one newton: hold a small apple in your hand. The pull of the Earth on it (its **weight**) is about 1 N.

<Tip>
Scientists measure forces with a **newton meter**: a spring inside a tube. The bigger the pull, the more the spring stretches, and a scale beside it shows the force in newtons.
</Tip>

## Two families of forces

**Contact forces** only act when two things touch:

- **friction**: the grip between surfaces that slows sliding;
- **air resistance**: air pushing back on anything moving through it;
- **a push or a pull** from your hands, a rope or a spring.

**Non-contact forces** act across a gap, with nothing touching:

- **gravity**: the pull between anything with mass, such as you and the Earth;
- **magnetic force**: a magnet pulling a paper clip before they touch;
- **electrostatic force**: a rubbed balloon pulling your hair upwards.

<Remember>
Force = a push or a pull. It has a **size** (in newtons, N) and a **direction**, so we draw it as an arrow.
</Remember>

## In the real world

<RealWorld title="Football">
When you kick the ball, your foot pushes it for only a fraction of a second. That one contact force is enough to send it flying. Then gravity (a non-contact force) pulls it back down to the ground.
</RealWorld>

<RealWorld title="The fridge magnet">
A magnet holds a drawing on the fridge door without glue. The magnetic force acts across the paper, so the magnet never touches the metal directly.
</RealWorld>

<RealWorld title="Opening a heavy door">
Door handles sit far from the hinges on purpose. The same push, in the right place and the right direction, turns the door much more easily.
</RealWorld>
